Nepal Gold Standard & Karat Guide

In Nepal, gold is traditionally measured in Tola (11.6638 grams) and classified under two major standards: Fine Gold (24K / Chhapawal) and Tejabi Gold (22K).

Fine Gold (24K) 99.9% Pure (Chhapawal)

Purest gold standard used for bullion coins, minting bars, and high-purity investment.

Tejabi Gold (22K) 91.67% Pure Gold

Widely used in Nepal for traditional handcrafted jewelry, wedding ornaments, and bangles.

18 Karat (18K) 75.0% Pure Gold

Popular for diamond-studded rings, modern delicate jewelry, and lightweight wear.

14 Karat (14K) 58.33% Pure Gold

Durable alloy resistant to daily wear and scratching, standard for daily accessories.

Nepal Precious Metal Weight Units

1 Tola = 11.6638 Grams (g)
1 Aana = 0.729 Grams (1/16 Tola)
1 Lal / Ratti = 0.1166 Grams (1/100 Tola)
1 Troy Ounce = 31.1035 Grams

Frequently Asked Questions (Nepal Market)

Gold price per Tola is calculated based on international spot market rates converted into USD/NPR exchange rate plus import duties set by the Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) and the Federation of Nepal Gold and Silver Dealers' Association (FENEGOSIDA).
1 Tola Gold Price (NPR) = (International Spot USD/Oz ÷ 31.1035 × 11.6638 × USD to NPR Exchange Rate) + Duty & Charges
Fine Gold (24K - Chhapawal) has a purity of 99.9% and carries the highest market value. Tejabi Gold (22K) has a purity of 91.67% and is typically made from recycled or alloyed gold used widely in handcrafted ornaments.
Making Charges (Jyala) is the artisan fee for crafting the ornament, while Wastage (Jhar) accounts for gold loss during melting and shaping. In Nepal, jewelers usually quote these as a percentage (typically 3%–10%) or a fixed rupee amount per Tola.
