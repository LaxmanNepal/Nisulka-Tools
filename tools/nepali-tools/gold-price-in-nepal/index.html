Gold Karat & Purity Reference Guide

The purity of gold is measured in Karats (K), which denotes the ratio of pure gold to other metal alloys (such as copper, silver, or zinc) out of 24 parts.

24 Karat (24K) 99.9% Pure Gold

Softest form, intense yellow luster. Used for investment bullion coins, bars, and high-purity minting.

22 Karat (22K) 91.67% Pure Gold

Standard across South Asia, Middle East, and Asia for fine handcrafted wedding jewelry.

18 Karat (18K) 75.0% Pure Gold

Ideal balance of durability and purity. Popular for diamond rings, luxury watches, and fine Western jewelry.

14 Karat (14K) 58.33% Pure Gold

Most common in the US and Europe for everyday durable jewelry resistant to bending and scratching.

Frequently Asked Questions

The spot price is the wholesale market price for raw, unprocessed metal traded on major global commodity exchanges (such as COMEX or LBMA). Retail prices include additional costs such as minting fees, wastage/making charges, transportation, insurance, dealer premiums, and local sales taxes.
Jewelry value is calculated using the formula:
Total Price = (Weight in Grams × Purity % × Live Rate per Gram) + Making Charges + Tax Our calculator automates this calculation for all karats and currencies instantly.
Precious metals are measured in Troy Ounces (t oz), an ancient weighing system dating back to Roman times. One troy ounce equals approximately 31.1035 grams, whereas a standard kitchen ounce (avoirdupois) equals 28.3495 grams.
